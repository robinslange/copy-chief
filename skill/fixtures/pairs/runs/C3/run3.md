Gate 0: clear. The draft makes no third-party claims. Findings, most severe first:

1. C2 (medium-high). Quoted lines: "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday" and "Book a drop-off on our website. You get a text with your box number within the hour."
   What fails: if a reader follows the call to action using only this page, they can't finish the job. (a) No shop address. "In Grey Lynn" is a suburb, not a door, so the reader has nowhere to drop the knives. (b) The two instructions are in the wrong order. The page first says to drop the knives in "the box", then later says a box number comes only after booking. A reader can't tell whether they must book first or can just turn up. (c) The page never says when or how payment happens: at booking, at the box, or at pickup. (d) The page never says how the knives come back on Friday, whether collection from the box or a return. (e) "On our website" names no link or button, and the reader is already on the website, so the place the click lands is undefined.
   Principle: Kennedy. The instructions must be unambiguous, and the reader must be able to say where the click lands, what arrives, and when.

2. C3 (medium). Quoted line: "Book a drop-off on our website."
   What fails: the only way to respond is a generic website booking. The page has no code for the buyer to quote, no dedicated or source-tagged link, and no page-specific number. The seller can't tell a booking that came from this page from one that came through search, word of mouth, or any other page.
   Principle: Hopkins, Kennedy. The response must be keyed and attributable.

3. O4 (low). Quoted line: "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday and they are back on Friday"
   What fails: this is the second direction of the check. The draft holds a real weekly cut-off (Tuesday in, Friday out) but never ties it to acting now. It doesn't say that missing Tuesday means waiting a week, or that the next Friday is the one on offer. A real reason to book now is sitting unused.
   Principle: Kennedy, and Cialdini on scarcity. A real reason to act now that the draft already holds should be used.

4. V2 (low). Quoted lines: "I sharpen kitchen knives by hand" against "Book a drop-off on our website."
   What fails: the voice is a single first-person tradesperson speaking to one reader, then it switches to the corporate plural "our" in the final call to action. That weakens the one-to-one address right at the close.
   Principle: Halbert. Address one reader, with no corporate plural.
