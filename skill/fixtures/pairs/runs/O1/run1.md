**Findings, most severe first**

1. **O1 (high).** Quoted line: none, because the price is missing.
   - What fails: I tried to fill "I get ___ for ___" from the draft. The first blank fills: "kitchen knives [sharpened] by hand on whetstones… back on Friday". The second blank has nothing in the draft to fill it. There is no price per knife, per box, or per visit.
   - The guarantee's "or refund you" also refunds an amount the page never states.
   - Principle: Kennedy. The reader has to be able to say what they get and what it costs.

2. **H3 (medium).** Quoted lines: "sharp enough to slice a tomato without pressing" against "If a knife is not sharper than the day you bought it, bring it back".
   - What fails: the headline and body promise one result, a tomato cut without pressing. Nothing in the draft pays that promise off with evidence, a demonstration, or a guarantee.
   - The guarantee covers a different, weaker, and vaguer standard: "sharper than the day you bought it". It doesn't say who judges that or how.
   - A knife that is sharper than new but still needs pressure is covered by neither the refund nor the promise.
   - Principle: Hopkins. A promise made has to be redeemed, and each claim is tracked separately.

3. **C1 (medium).** Quoted lines: "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday" and "Book a drop-off at knives.example/drop and quote code TOMATO."
   - What fails: there are two imperatives, and the draft never says which comes first. They can be read as two alternative routes.
   - "the box at the shop door" (one shared box) conflicts with "your box number" (an allocated box).
   - A reader who just walks up and drops their knives skips the booking and the code. That also breaks the attribution that C3 otherwise gets from TOMATO.
   - Principle: Kennedy. There should be one ask, or one clearly sequenced path.

4. **X2 (low to medium).** Quoted line: "they are back on Friday, sharp enough to slice a tomato without pressing".
   - What fails: this is an unconditional performance outcome, not a fulfilment term. "Back on Friday" is fulfilment. "Sharp enough to…" promises a result for every knife dropped off, with no hedge, including chipped, serrated, or badly damaged blades.
   - The seller's own guarantee backs a lower standard than this promise, so the promise is pitched stronger than the seller is willing to stand behind.
   - Principle: substantiation. A promised outcome has to be one the seller can support before making it. Under X2's test this sits at the edge, because it is the service's effect on the knife rather than on the reader.

5. **M1 (low).** Quoted line: "which is how the cut on your thumb happened."
   - What fails: the draft states as fact an injury most first-time visitors have not had. The skating-off-the-tomato moment is a state a visitor plausibly arrives in. The definite "the cut on your thumb" is not.
   - A reader without a cut reads a false claim about themselves in the second sentence.
   - Principle: Schwartz on awareness. The copy should address the state the reader is actually in.

6. **P3 (low).** Quoted line: "I sharpen kitchen knives by hand on whetstones in Grey Lynn."
   - What fails: the method is named, but no chain from method to result is given. The draft never says what hand sharpening on whetstones does that produces the tomato cut, or why it beats whatever dulled the knife or whatever the reader would do instead.
   - The strongest "because" I can build from the draft is the method's name.
   - Principle: the Schwartz lineage on unique mechanism.
