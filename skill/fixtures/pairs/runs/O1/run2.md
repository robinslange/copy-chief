Principle (Kennedy): the reader has to be able to say what they get and what it costs using only the page.

2. C2 (medium). The lines "Book a drop-off at knives.example/drop" and "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door" leave several things unsaid:
   - The page never gives the shop's address. "In Grey Lynn" names a suburb, not a door.
   - It never says what the booking link opens or where TOMATO gets entered.
   - It never says when or how payment happens.
   - The order is unclear. The drop instruction comes before the booking instruction, and "your box number" arrives only after booking, so a reader can't tell whether they can drop knives without booking first.
   Principle (Kennedy): the reader should be able to follow the call to action and describe where the click lands, what arrives and when, from the page alone.

3. P3 (medium). The line "I sharpen kitchen knives by hand on whetstones" is the draft's only answer to why a knife comes back "sharper than the day you bought it". It names a method but never explains how the method produces the result. Nothing says what hand whetstone work does that the factory edge or a pull-through sharpener doesn't, so the strongest claim on the page has no because. Principle (Schwartz lineage on unique mechanism): a claim needs a chain of the form "this does that, which produces the result".

4. X2 (low). The line "they are back on Friday, sharp enough to slice a tomato without pressing" is stated as a certain outcome for every knife, including damaged or cheap ones. The guarantee covers a different standard, "not sharper than the day you bought it", so the tomato promise has no remedy attached. Principle (substantiation end of claim posture): a promised outcome has to be one the seller can hold for every buyer, or be hedged. Since the result concerns the knife rather than the buyer's body, the risk is small.

5. O4 (low, the unused-reason direction). The line "Drop your knives ... by Tuesday and they are back on Friday" sets up a weekly cut-off, but the draft never ties it to acting now. Principle (Kennedy; Cialdini on scarcity): a real time-bound fact the draft already holds and doesn't use is a missed reason to act.

Visitors with a dull knife arrive problem-aware, which the headline catches, but many won't have a cut. For them this line is false about their own week, and it can break the recognition the headline built. Principle (Schwartz awareness states): the copy should address the state a first-time visitor is actually in.
