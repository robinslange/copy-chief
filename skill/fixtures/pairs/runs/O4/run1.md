Findings, most severe first:

   What fails: nothing on the page says why the deadline exists or what the offer is. No sentence says NZ$18 is a reduced price, what the price is after Sunday, or why Sunday ends it. 2. C2 (medium). Quoted lines: "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday" and "Book a drop-off at knives.example/drop and quote code TOMATO. You get a text with your box number within the hour."
   What fails: the reader can't carry out the physical step from the page. The shop door has no address or street, only "in Grey Lynn". The page tells the reader to drop knives off before it tells them to book, but the box number only arrives after booking. "The box" (one box) doesn't match "your box number" (numbered boxes). The page never says how the knives come back on Friday (collect from the box? delivered?) or when payment happens. The online steps (click, text, within the hour) are clear. The offline steps are not.
   Principle: instructions must be unambiguous (Kennedy). The reader has to be able to say where they go, what happens and when.

So "sharp enough to slice a tomato without pressing" rests on the method's name alone.
   Principle: name the mechanism, the unique-mechanism idea that runs back to Schwartz. A claim needs a because.
