Findings, most severe first:

1. O4 (high): "Offer ends Sunday at midnight."
   This deadline is made up. Nothing in the draft says why the offer ends on Sunday. The draft also never says what "the offer" is. The only prices given are NZ$18 and NZ$24, and nothing says they go up after Sunday or what they go up to. So the deadline carries no stated consequence. The draft does hold a real time-bound fact it could have tied to acting now: the weekly cycle, "by Tuesday … back on Friday". That fact is used only as logistics. If NZ$18 is actually the normal price, a deadline on it in New Zealand also risks a misleading price representation under the Fair Trading Act.
   Principle: Kennedy, and Cialdini on scarcity. Urgency needs a stated reason, and a real reason the draft already has should be used.

2. C2 (medium): "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday" / "Book a drop-off at knives.example/drop and quote code TOMATO. You get a text with your box number within the hour."
   Working only from the page, the reader cannot complete the path:
   (a) There is no shop address. Grey Lynn is a suburb, not a door.
   (b) The order of steps is unclear. The first instruction says to drop knives in "the box" before booking is mentioned. The last paragraph implies numbered boxes that you only get after booking. A reader following the first paragraph could drop knives without booking.
   (c) The draft never says when or how payment happens.
   (d) It is unclear whether booking by Sunday but dropping off on Tuesday still gets the offer.
   Principle: Kennedy. The reader must be able to describe exactly what happens next: where, what arrives, and when.

3. P3 (medium): "I sharpen kitchen knives by hand on whetstones in Grey Lynn."
   Asked "why does this work?", the draft offers only the name of the method. There is no chain of cause and effect: nothing on why the edge went dull, or on what hand sharpening on a whetstone does that the reader's current option does not. "By hand on whetstones" is the only thing that sets the service apart, and it is left as a label instead of an explanation.
   Principle: the Schwartz line on unique mechanism. A claim needs a "because".

4. H3 (low to medium): "sharp enough to slice a tomato without pressing" vs "If a knife is not sharper than the day you bought it, bring it back".
   The body's main promise is the tomato test, but the guarantee covers a different standard: sharper than the day of purchase. The reader cannot check that standard, because they have no record of how sharp the knife was on that day. So the guarantee does not back the promise the page makes. Nothing else supports the tomato promise with evidence either, such as a demonstration or a count of knives done. The guarantee also leaves open whether the seller or the buyer chooses between "redo it free" and "refund you".
   Principle: Hopkins. Every promise the hook makes must be paid off in the body, and each promise is tracked separately.
