**Findings, most severe first**

2. **C2, medium (next step unclear).** Quoted lines: "Drop your knives in the box at the shop door by Tuesday" and "Book a drop-off at knives.example/drop … You get a text with your box number within the hour."

     - The page describes dropping knives off before it mentions booking, so the order of the two steps is unclear. The box number only arrives after booking.
   - Principle: Kennedy. Instructions must be unambiguous, and the reader must be able to do the next step using only the page.

3. **P3, low (method named, but no reason it works).** Quoted line: "I sharpen kitchen knives by hand on whetstones in Grey Lynn."
   - What fails: Asked "why does this work?", the draft can only answer "by hand on whetstones". That names a method but doesn't explain how it gets the result. Nothing says what hand work on a whetstone does that other sharpening doesn't, or why the edge comes back good enough for a tomato. Any hand sharpener could write the same sentence.
   - Principle: the Schwartz line on unique mechanism, meaning a claim needs a "because".

4. **M1, low (claims something that may not have happened to the reader).** Quoted line: "you press harder, which is how the cut on your thumb happened."
   - What fails: The problem-aware opening fits: the knife skating off a tomato is something the reader has probably seen this week. But the thumb cut is stated as fact, and many first-time visitors won't have one. For them, the draft asserts an event that never happened in the second sentence of the body, which weakens the otherwise accurate opening.
   - Principle: Schwartz on awareness states. The copy should meet readers in the state they actually arrive in.
