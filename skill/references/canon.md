# Canon

Ten mechanisms. Each names the checks that enforce it and the vault notes that
go deeper. This is a routing table, not a corpus — the depth lives in the vault.

## 1. Awareness
The reader arrives in one of five states, from unaware to most-aware, and copy
written for the wrong state reads as jargon.
Owner: Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966).
Enforced by: M1.
Vault: `awareness-stage-mismatch-burns-copy-budget-on-solo-studios`.

## 2. Market sophistication
Once a market has heard a claim, repeating it stops working, and the claim
either moves to mechanism or the copy wins through restraint instead.
Owner: Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), five sophistication stages.
Enforced by: M2, P3.
Vault: `restraint-reads-as-confidence-in-a-claim-saturated-category`.

## 3. Desire
Copy channels a desire the reader already walked in with; it cannot manufacture
one from nothing.
Owner: Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966).
Enforced by: M4.
Vault: `copy-cannot-create-desire-only-channel-it`.

## 4. The offer
The offer outranks the words describing it — a starving market forgives weak
copy, and the value equation weighs what the reader gets against what it costs
them to get it.
Owner: Halbert, starving crowd. Hormozi, value equation (dream outcome,
perceived likelihood, time delay, effort — the exact wording of the second term
is unsettled between sources and is not asserted here).
Enforced by: O1, O2.
Vault: `the-starving-crowd-outranks-the-copy`,
`the-value-equation-has-four-terms-and-copy-usually-addresses-one`.

## 5. Hook
The headline carries a specific promise or opens a specific gap, the first
line's only job is to earn the second, and each element sells the next down the
slide.
Owner: Caples and Ogilvy on the headline's weight, Sugarman on the slippery
slide, Hopkins on the claim getting paid off.
Enforced by: H1, H2, H3, H4.
Vault: `hero-lede-resolves-the-h1-not-restates-it`,
`name-the-failure-moment-not-the-value-relationship`,
`name-the-feared-moment-not-the-feature-in-conversion-subheads`,
`every-element-of-an-ad-exists-to-get-the-next-element-read`.

## 6. Proof and specificity
Specific claims beat general ones, every argumentative claim needs evidence
behind it, and an unsourced statistic, study, or named attribute blocks the
draft outright.
Owner: Hopkins, specific over general, test everything.
Enforced by: P1, P2, Gate 0.
Vault: `concrete-language-recall-is-4-to-10x-not-a-taste-question`,
`specificity-is-the-bridge-between-stopping-power-and-personality`,
`supplement-claim-conversion-hierarchy`,
`cite-the-trial-not-the-pathway-on-supplement-receipt-lines`.

## 7. Unique mechanism
In a sophisticated market a bare claim gets ignored; naming *why* it works is
what makes the claim survivable.
Owner: Schwartz lineage.
Enforced by: P3.
Vault: `visceral-first-mechanism-second-for-supplement-copy`,
`convert-a-falsifiable-uniqueness-claim-into-an-unrefutable-process-claim`.

## 8. Influence
Six compliance levers in *Influence* (1984) move a reader toward yes, with
unity added later through *Pre-Suasion* (2016) and subsequent editions, making
seven in the current count. Their empirical support is uneven and is stated
per-principle, never as one blanket verdict for the book: descriptive-norm
social proof has a documented failed replication, door-in-the-face is
significant for verbal and not for behavioural compliance, scarcity survives
meta-analysis as product-type-moderated, and the priming-adjacent material is
weaker than all of them.
Owner: Cialdini, *Influence* (1984), *Pre-Suasion* (2016).
Enforced by: O4, P4.
Vault: `cialdinis-seven-levers-do-not-carry-equal-evidential-weight`.

## 9. Voice
Voice means Whitman's Life Force 8 reaching one named reader in Halbert's
sense, never a committee.
Owner: Whitman, Life Force 8. Halbert, one reader.
Enforced by: M3, V1, V2, V3, V4.
Vault: `concrete-language-recall-is-4-to-10x-not-a-taste-question` (V1),
`the-life-force-eight-are-the-wants-copy-does-not-have-to-argue-for`.

## 10. Close and measurement
The close asks for one action, moves the risk onto the seller instead of the
buyer, and keys the response so the piece can prove it produced the result.
Owner: Kennedy, one CTA. Abraham, risk reversal. Hopkins, keyed measurement.
Enforced by: C1, C2, C3, O3.
Vault: `risk-reversal-moves-the-buyers-remaining-objection-onto-the-seller`,
`an-unkeyed-response-cannot-be-attributed-so-it-cannot-be-tested`.

## Compliance — deliberately unrouted

X1 and X2 have no section above. That is not a gap in the ten mechanisms; it
is the correct shape. X1 and X2 are the compliance pair, and compliance is
regulatory rather than canonical — no direct-response figure owns
jurisdictional claim posture, the FTC, DSHEA, and NZ DSR 1985 do. checks.md
carries their full attribution: X1 to the jurisdictional claim-posture family
(NZ DSR 1985, US DSHEA, Canadian NPN), X2 to FTC substantiation doctrine and
the vault notes `msm-compliant-claim-framing-for-nz-performance-supplement`
and `a-supplement-headline-claim-usually-outruns-its-human-evidence`.
